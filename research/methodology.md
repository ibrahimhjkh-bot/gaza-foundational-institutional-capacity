# Gaza Sustainable Governance — Research Protocol (public working version)

Date: 2026-11-01. Status: preliminary analytical research; not peer-reviewed or field-validated.

## Questions
1. Macro: which political, legal, fiscal, access, and ecological conditions govern feasible public-service mandates?
2. Meso: which staffing, management, finance, and coordination capabilities are necessary within institutions?
3. Micro: what service outcomes, safeguards, and citizen feedback demonstrate fair and sustainable performance?

## Source approach
Prioritize original publishers and institutional reports, distinguish report publication date from period of observed data, and keep one entry for each new source in evidence-register.csv. Sources are purposively selected; this is not an exhaustive systematic review. Historical studies establish mechanisms, not current status. International empirical findings are comparators, not Gaza causal estimates.

## Claim discipline
- Fact: quote or paraphrase grounded in identifiable source and time period.
- Interpretation: transparent inference connecting facts to research questions.
- Proposal: untested possible design, conditional on legal authority, access and locally representative review.

## Example baseline
Gaza RDNA (World Bank/UN/EU April 2026): USD 71.4bn estimated 10-year recovery needs; USD 26.3bn for the first 18 months. Ratio 26.3/71.4 = 36.8347% (approx 36.8%); this is a share of estimated needs, not committed funding. RDNA estimates refer to damages through October 2025 and themselves caution that follow-up on-the-ground sectoral surveys are required.

## Red lines
Do not present proposed metrics as observations, monetary needs as pledged money, study associations as causal estimates, or formal committees as operational unless current evidence supports this. Record opposing interpretations and constraints, especially political authority, access, security, and rights.

## Next research tasks
Primary-source legal analysis of transitional mandate; structured interviews when safely feasible; updated municipal/sector datasets; interviews with water and environmental-service professionals; survey methods for representative community voice; blind external expert review.
